Prototype name *Sen'fi*; where `sen` lit. means writing or glyphs and `fi` means hearing or sense: ᴠɪʙᴇ-ɢʟʏᴘʜꜱ — writing what one hears or feels.
Longer name: *Hojasen'fi* (~ the language of glyphs and sounds -> the language of equal sounds and meanings)
### Overview
Welcome to SEN'FI, a passion project of mine to make an artlang (conlang made for aesthetic purposes) restricted to having **as many meanings as it has syllables**, written like Sitelen Pona and Kanji. Glyphs are intuitive and pictographic/ideographic, basing most of their etymology on **old Chinese scripts**. Sounds are **symbolic, onomatopoeic and/or loanwords**. Vocabulary is mostly **compounds** of said core logosyllables; and aims to be **as non-ambiguous as possible** unlike Toki Pona.

---
### Objective
##### LOGOSYLLABIC
Every singular logogram is monosyllabic and there are no homophones (except for q-starting and vowel-starting syllables), so one core meaning is attached to only one syllable. There can be multiple kanji spelt the same in Japanese, but in this language there is no confusion. And the kanji equivalent, i.e. stacked words, are pronounced using their components in the order left to right downwards. This should theoretically make learning vocabulary easier. Such core meanings are called "[[Sen'fi/Vocab - Atomic Radicals\|Atomic radicals]]" as they function like building blocks, whereas the constructions are called "[[Sen'fi/Vocab - Compounds\|Compounds]]".
##### INTELLECTUAL CHALLENGE — LIMITED LOGOSYLLABLE INVENTORY
Basically a linguistic and intellectual game where I have a limited amount of core meanings, equal to the amount of possible syllables (which cannot have codas besides "n", that is roughly 400) and I have to wisely choose what "radicals" are able to describe pretty much everything. Besides my limited meaning-syllable inventory, I also need to take care that compound words are not tediously long to spell. I intend to maintain this language throughout my life as a passion project.
##### SOUND SYMBOLISM, IMITATION & LOANWORDS
Most of the radicals' sounds come from "sound symbolism" (consonants and vowels working together to intuitively give the idea of the meaning) and onomatopoeia/imitation. The rest originate from Japanese and other languages.
##### PRIMARILY CHINESE SCRIPT BASED
Most of the radicals' glyphs come from my obsessive research on the origins of CJK (Chinese-Japanese-Korean) logographic characters and my interpretations of them. This is a win-win for me as it coincides with my obsession with Japanese and hence its original Chinese glyphs. Here are the sources I use as reference:
- [Zi.tools](https://zi.tools/?secondary=explore) - Online Chinese script evolution charts
- [Brad Warden's Kanji Etymology](https://bradwarden.com/kanji/etymology/) - Brad Warden's interpretation on Kanji
- ChatGPT - When in doubt about etymology sources
##### TOKI PONA BASED (BUT NOT EXACTLY)
While I may take inspiration from my first conlang Toki Pona, often dubbed "the Simplest Language in the world", I don't want to make a photocopy of it. Rather, I want to give myself a similar challenge to restrict myself to a tiny base-vocabulary from which I then build up non-ambiguous compound nouns. This is unlike Toki Pona which always stresses on ambiguity in speech, depending a lot on context.

### Glyph aesthetic
- Angular and/or curvy, hieroglyphic / oracle bone script (so radicals should be mostly pictograms), kanji-like in structure.
- Words can be written either like kanji / Hangeul formations (stacking symbols onto each other, read from left to right downwards), or as plain successive symbols without stacking, or a mix of both(?).
- Direction of writing: Japanese direction (top-down-leftward) or English direction (left-right-downward)
### Etymology
- For glyph inspiration, refer to [[Sen'fi/About#PRIMARILY CHINESE SCRIPT BASED\|aforementioned Chinese etymology]].
- Try to avoid making overly complex etymologies and words
	- For a specific word like "dog" which is a `ga` (animal) but cannot be perfectly described in brief, get the imitative sound and/or loanword (in this case Japanese onomatopoeia "wanwan") and mark it with `the`: `wan-the-ga` (the `ga` that goes "wan"). Such loanword or onomatopoeia is written using the special [[Sen'fi/Phonotactics & Alphabet\|alphabet]], grammatically acting like Katakana (written in a Hangeul style).
		- `the` is vital to the language because it tells the listener that the preceding sounds are not from the language, they are from other languages and onomatopoeia; thus allowing me to break the rule of syllables strictly meaning one thing. `the` words act like Katakana in Japanese, meanwhile `tho` acts like "tte" or "to".
		- Such `the` words are usually compliant to this language's phonotactics (i.e. using only the syllables provided) however in case of absolute necessity phonotactics may be broken, such as `oktantheho`(the `ho` of "Octantis" -> South).
- Etymologies are based on concrete things, but also based on metaphors or other abstract/philosophical sources when necessary
- Wordbuilding is not a concern for now
	- Etymologies can come from both primitive knowledge and modern scientific knowledge
- This may come across as lazy but sometimes when a word which is strictly grammatical carries a significant semantic space in its glyph, meaning, and/or sound, for example: 
	- `o` ("and"), the "tse" glyph (refer to [[Sen'fi/Vocab - Atomic Radicals#Grammar\|Grammar section]]) is used to convert such a word to a noun by lengthening its vowel -> `oo`, meaning "addition".
	- `e` ("or"), likewise, -> `ee`, meaning "alternative".
	- `shi` ("what"), likewise, -> `shii`, meaning "unknown".
	- `dun` ("on, at"), likewise, -> `duun`, meaning "existence".
	This aims to prevent confusing a grammatical particle with a semantic noun. In the conlang which inspired my conlang, Toki Pona, there is controversial minor use of `anu` ("or") to mean "choice"; I aim to mirror this and offer a solution in my equivalent. Controversial "nasin" aside, Toki Pona already has particles which also act as nouns, such as `lon`, meaning either "on, at" or "existence".
- Despite the previous point, for the sake of convenience sometimes `tse` can be dropped in casual speech and writing, such as `duntsezhin` and `dunzhin`.
### Order of nouns and modifiers
- Toki Pona head nouns: e.g. "wing" is "arm of bird" not "bird arm"
- Non-toki pona; kanji-like constructions (sometimes) e.g. "person + leg" means "movement", not "person of leg"
### Plurality
- Monosyllabic words such as `me` are pluralised by reduplication: `meme`
- However for convenience of speech and writing, reduplication is replaced with `tsa` or `tso`.
	- `tsa` pluralises a subword within a compound word
	- `tso` pluralises the whole word
